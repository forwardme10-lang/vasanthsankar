Two hats, one head

Designer and product manager are the same job seen from two chairs.

One asks "what should this feel like?" and the other asks "is this worth building, and when?" I move between them freely, which keeps the roadmap honest and the screens grounded in something real.

As a designer

I draw to understand.

Research, flows, interface, and the words on the screen. I care about the second-week experience as much as the first-minute one, and I prototype early enough that the team can argue with something real.

  • Research
  • Flows
  • UI systems
  • Prototyping
  • UX writing
As a product manager

I decide what not to build.

Problem framing, prioritisation, metrics, and the unglamorous work of saying no kindly. I write the one-pager before the backlog, and I keep a close loop between what we shipped and what the numbers say.

  • Roadmaps

How I build from scratch

A route, not a process diagram.

Every product I've started has followed roughly this line. The order matters, the pace doesn't. Some waypoints take an afternoon, some take a quarter, and I always loop back to the start.

  1. Waypoint 01

    Notice

    Sit with users, support tickets, and my own annoyance. Write down what people do, not what they say they want.

  2. Waypoint 02

    Frame

    One page: who, what hurts, why now, how we'll know. If I can't write it, we're not ready to design it.

  3. Waypoint 03

    Sketch

    Paper and ugly wireframes. Ten directions, fast, before falling in love with any of them.

  4. Waypoint 04

    Prototype

    Something clickable in days, in front of five real people. Cut what nobody touches.

  5. Waypoint 05

    Ship

    Smallest honest version to a small group. Instrument it. Watch the first-week numbers like a hawk.

  6. Waypoint 06

    Learn

    What surprised us? Fold it back into the one-pager and ride the loop again.

Field notes

What the outdoors keeps teaching me.

  • Trails are great onboarding. A good trail never explains itself. It just makes the next step obvious.
  • Gearing beats power. The right ratio for the gradient wins. The right scope for the team does too.
  • Weather is a stakeholder. You plan around it and still carry a jacket.
  • Pack light, carry a repair kit. Ship small, keep the ability to fix things fast.
